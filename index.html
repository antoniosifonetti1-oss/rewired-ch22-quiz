Rewired · Chapter 22

The art of developing agentic AI solutions

Study the key ideas, then test yourself with a 5-question quiz.

1Match agents to the right work

Agents are not a yes/no choice; there is a spectrum from traditional automation to agents led by humans (Exhibit 22.2). LLMs excel at summarizing, drafting, classifying, extracting and brainstorming, and struggle with precise multistep math, ambiguity and deterministic, auditable decisions (Exhibit 22.1).

  • Good fit: repetitive high-volume workflows, retrieval and synthesis at scale, orchestration across systems, decision support under clear constraints.
  • Poor fit: ambiguous or strategic decisions, empathy and relationship building, tacit or physical knowledge, high regulatory, legal or safety risk.

2Constrain agents and feed them good context

Build agents and guardrails at the task level, not the global solution level: break the work down as much as possible and give each agent clear instructions. Then pick the right technique for the input:

  • Prompt engineering: improves quality without touching the model.
  • Context engineering: controls everything the model sees (instructions, roles, memory, retrieved data, token budget).
  • RAG: fetches authoritative, changing content (e.g. internal policy).
  • Fine-tuning: a scalpel, not a hammer. Use it for narrow, stable behaviors, not for content that changes often.

3Test early and often

Agents are non-deterministic, so the rule of thumb is one-third building, two-thirds testing. New risks include nondeterministic outputs, emergent behaviors, a larger attack surface (prompt injection, jailbreaks, data exfiltration) and autonomous actions.

  • Define scope, measurable standards and testable red lines; build a high-quality test dataset.
  • Layer your tests: static review → LLM-as-judge → human review → live shadow mode.
  • Test the whole product (latency, handoffs), then monitor continuously with rollback plans.

4Decide buy vs. build, and design for reuse

Simple task automation is widely available from vendors; complex multi-workflow systems are a source of differentiation and usually need a mix of off-the-shelf and custom agents. Assemble atomic agents (one specific task each) under orchestrator and compliance agents, and host them on a central platform so they can be reused.

  • Six buy/build principles: value of IP, expediency, reusability, total cost of ownership, customization load, modularity.
  • Focus on how agents communicate, not where they live; share core capabilities but decentralize agent builds; prioritize context quality.
Company example

Healthcare claims adjudication

Adjudicating a claim means checking claim type, patient history, policies, medical necessity and fraud signals. A single agent doing all of that would pile up reasoning errors, burn tokens and leave no trail to debug. Instead, each task gets its own agent (Exhibit 22.3):

Orchestrator→ Policy compliance· Fraud detection· Medical necessity→ Human specialist review→ Payment + communication
Each task can be scoped, tested and observed on its own, humans review at the right points, and agents (e.g. policy compliance) can be reused in other workflows.
